Three Puerto Rican bartenders have each claimed the drink. The Caribe Hilton
credits Ramón "Monchito" Marrero, who tended its Beachcomber Bar and says he
combined rum, pineapple juice and the newly available canned cream of coconut
in 1954 after being asked to create a new welcome drink for guests
<Cite ref="hilton"/>. Marrero himself dated the moment two years earlier, to
August 1952, and a colleague at the same bar, Ricardo Garcia, has separately
claimed the invention for himself <Cite ref="difford"/>. A third claim comes
from Ramón Portas Mingot, who says he made the drink in 1963 at the Barrachina
Restaurant in Old San Juan, which still displays a plaque to that effect
<Cite ref="difford"/>.

None of the three claims is independently documented from the time, and the
drink does not appear in print in a cocktail book until the late 1960s
<Cite ref="difford"/>. A related, coconut-free drink combining rum, pineapple
and lime was already being served under the same name in the 1920s, closer to
a shaken pineapple Daiquiri than to the blended drink known today
<Cite ref="difford"/>. What the competing bar claims agree on is the setting:
San Juan, the 1950s and 60s, and the sweetened, canned cream of coconut that
made the modern proportions possible.

The dispute was eventually settled administratively rather than historically.
In 1978, Puerto Rico's legislature declared the Piña Colada the island's
official drink <Cite ref="hilton"/>, without adjudicating which bar, or which
bartender, made the first one.
