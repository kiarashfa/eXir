Fratelli Branca began exporting Fernet-Branca to Argentina in 1907 and
opened a distillery in Buenos Aires in 1925, well ahead of the drink this
page describes <Cite ref="licorea"/>. The combination with cola is generally
placed in Córdoba province, though its exact first mixing is not documented;
accounts range from the 1950s to a specific 1970s attribution to a Cruz del
Eje bartender, and none of them can be checked against a contemporary
source <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

What is documented is the scale it reached. Córdoba consumes about
thirty per cent of the bitters sold in Argentina, more than any other
province and enough to make it the heaviest per-capita market for the drink
anywhere <Cite ref="licorea"/>. Fratelli Branca's own Argentine marketing
ran an implicit, then formal, co-branding campaign pairing Fernet with cola
between 1994 and 1997, a campaign credited with helping consumption grow by
251% over the 2000s against a much smaller rise for beer over the same
period <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. Argentina now accounts for more than
three-quarters of the fernet produced worldwide <Cite ref="licorea"/>.

The drink was recognised as an official IBA cocktail in 2020, under the name
Fernandito, in the organisation's "new era drinks" category — the first
Argentine drink to receive that recognition <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.
