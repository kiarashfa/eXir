The story behind blending fruit with leafy greens rather than only with each
other is told by one source: Victoria Boutenko, a raw food author who says she
arrived at the combination in 2004 while looking for a way to eat more dark
leafy greens than she could manage by chewing them <Cite ref="raw"/>. By her
own account, blending greens on their own produced a drink she found
unpalatable, and the idea to add fruit came from reading that chimpanzees,
whose diet researchers had studied for its similarity to a healthy human one,
were sometimes observed wrapping fruit in a leaf before eating it
<Cite ref="raw"/>. Blending banana with kale, in her telling, produced
something that smelled and tasted like an ordinary fruit smoothie despite the
greens in it.

No independent account of this moment exists outside Boutenko's own writing,
which is worth stating plainly rather than repeating the story as settled
history. What is independently verifiable is what came after it: Boutenko
published the recipes and reasoning in *Green for Life* in 2005, a book that
went on to a finalist nomination for an Independent Publisher's Award the
following year and later editions <Cite ref="penguin"/>. The book, not a
single blended glass, is what actually carried the format from one kitchen
into wider circulation, on the strength of its claimed health benefits for
digestion and nutrient absorption rather than any culinary novelty.
