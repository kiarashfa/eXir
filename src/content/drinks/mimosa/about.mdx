The Mimosa is usually credited to Frank Meier, head bartender at the Ritz
Hotel in Paris, and dated to the mid-1920s, though sources differ on the
exact year, placing it anywhere from 1921 to 1925 <Cite ref="difford"/>. The
attribution itself is not solid: Meier's own 1936 cocktail book marked every
recipe he claimed to have invented with a symbol, and the Mimosa carries no
such mark, despite his willingness to claim credit for other drinks in the
same book <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

A near-identical drink, the Buck's Fizz, was already being served in London
by 1921, made by bartender Malachi "Pat" McGarry at Buck's Club
<Cite ref="difford"/>. The two are distinguished mainly by ratio: a Buck's
Fizz is built two parts sparkling wine to one part orange juice and served
without ice, while a Mimosa is built in equal parts and served over ice in
some accounts <Cite ref="difford"/>. Given that the Buck's Fizz is the
earlier of the two by several years, it is treated by some sources as the
Mimosa's direct predecessor rather than a separate, coincidentally similar
drink <Cite ref="difford"/>; other accounts stop short of stating a
descent and note only that they are frequently confused with each other
<Cite ref="wiki"/>.

The name itself is unconnected to either origin story: it refers to the
bright yellow flowers of *Acacia dealbata*, commonly called mimosa, which
the drink's colour was thought to resemble <Cite ref="wiki"/>. Less credible
accounts crediting the film director Alfred Hitchcock with popularising the
drink in 1940s San Francisco are not supported by the earlier Paris and
London dates already on record <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
