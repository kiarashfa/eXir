Julio Bermejo, whose parents Elmy and Tommy opened Tommy's Mexican
Restaurant in San Francisco in 1965, is credited with the recipe from
around 1990, sources differing slightly on whether it was that year or the
tail end of the 1980s <Cite ref="wiki"/> <Cite ref="punch"/>. Bermejo made
two changes to a standard Margarita: he moved from mixto tequila to a
100% agave bottling, and he replaced the orange liqueur and sugar syrup
with agave syrup, a sweetener he had encountered through California health
food producers rather than through bartending <Cite ref="punch"/>. The
pairing was as much conceptual as technical — tequila and agave syrup share
the same plant — and it let the tequila's own character carry the drink
rather than being covered by a second spirit <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Bermejo also dropped the salted rim that had become standard on a
Margarita, reasoning that it had developed largely to soften the rougher
edges of a mixto tequila and was unnecessary once the tequila itself was
100% agave <Cite ref="punch"/>.

The drink's spread beyond the restaurant ran through individual
bartenders rather than publication. Tony Abou-Ganim brought Bermejo in as a
consultant at the Bellagio in Las Vegas, and British bartenders Dre Masso
and Henry Besant encountered the drink at Tommy's in 2003 and carried it
into bars across Europe and beyond through their consultancy work
<Cite ref="punch"/>. By 2008 it had gone from one restaurant's house pour
to the first venue-specific cocktail added to the International
Bartenders Association's official list <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
