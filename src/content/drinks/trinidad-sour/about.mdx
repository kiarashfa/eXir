The bartender Giuseppe Gonzalez created the Trinidad Sour in 2009, during an
early stint at Clover Club, the Brooklyn cocktail bar that had opened the
previous year <Cite ref="wiki"/>. He entered it in a competition that same
year; it did not place in the top ten <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

The drink's defining move is proportional rather than technical: Angostura
bitters, normally added by the dash, is poured here as the largest single
ingredient in the glass, standing in for the base spirit a sour would
usually be built on <Cite ref="punch"/>. Orgeat's almond sweetness and
lemon's acidity are built to meet that concentration of bitters halfway,
with a small measure of rye behind both of them for structure rather than
flavour <Cite ref="punch"/>. Angostura is not sold or priced as a mixing
spirit, and pouring it by the measure rather than the dash made the drink
markedly more expensive to make than an ordinary sour, which was itself part
of what made it stand out at the time <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Despite its poor competition showing, the recipe spread by word of mouth
among bartenders over the following years and was eventually adopted as an
official cocktail by the International Bartenders Association
<Cite ref="wiki"/>.
