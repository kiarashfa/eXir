The drink was created by Jörg Meyer at Le Lion, his bar in Hamburg, after a
trip to New York in the winter of 2007 where he tasted Dale DeGroff's Whiskey
Smash at the Pegu Club <Cite ref="punch"/>. Back in Hamburg the following
year, a gin recipe booklet that garnished its serve with basil sent him
toward the herb, and by the summer of 2008 he was shaking gin, lemon and
sugar with a handful of it muddled in <Cite ref="punch"/>.

Meyer served the drink under the name Gin Pesto before settling on its
current one <Cite ref="wiki"/>. He marks 10 July 2008, the day he posted the
recipe to the Bitters Blog he co-wrote with Stephan Berg, as its real
birthday <Cite ref="punch"/>. The post spread through the European bartending
community within a single summer, helped by the Traveling Mixologists, a
touring group of bartenders formed a few years earlier, and the drink is
credited as the first modern cocktail to reach worldwide fame from a European
bar outside London or Paris <Cite ref="punch"/>.

It has since been adopted by the International Bartenders Association as one
of its official cocktails, in the category the IBA reserves for recent
additions to the canon rather than the pre-Prohibition list <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
Le Lion itself leaned into the association: Meyer had "Cradle of the Gin
Basil Smash" painted on the bar's exterior in 2012 <Cite ref="punch"/>. The
smash format it belongs to — a spirit, citrus and sugar sour with a muddled
herb worked through it — is older and mostly whiskey-based in America; this
is the gin version that outgrew its source.
