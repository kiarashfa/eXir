Heated spiced wine reached Britain by the same Roman route it reached
Germany and Scandinavia by: legions carried both wine and the practice of
warming it north as far as the Scottish borders <Cite ref="wiki"/>. An
English cookbook of 1390, *The Forme of Cury*, already gives a recipe
combining red wine and sugar with cinnamon, ginger, galangal, cloves and
nutmeg, a spice list close to what a British mulled wine still uses six
centuries later <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Unlike Germany's Gluhwein, which settled into a comparatively fixed formula
tied to Christmas markets, British mulled wine never fixed on one recipe.
Modern versions are commonly built from some combination of orange, lemon,
cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, cardamom, ginger and either fennel seed or star
anise, without any single combination treated as the standard one
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. It is conventionally served in small cups, around two
hundred millilitres, often porcelain or glass, and frequently finished with
a clove-studded orange slice floated on top <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Its older English name, hippocras, points at how the drink was originally
understood: not as a festive treat but as a health tonic, taken hot or cold
<Cite ref="conversation"/>. Chaucer has a character in *The Canterbury
Tales* drink "ypocras, clarre, and vernage" of hot spices specifically for
its restorative effect, and the word "mulled" itself is traced to the Old
English *mulse*, rooted in the Latin for honey, from a period before sugar
was the sweetener of choice <Cite ref="conversation"/>. The drink's tie to
Christmas specifically is a later, and comparatively recent, association:
one account credits Charles Dickens's 1843 *A Christmas Carol*, which
features a mulled-wine variant called Smoking Bishop made with port and
dried oranges, with fixing the drink in the popular idea of an English
Christmas <Cite ref="conversation"/>. Modern British variants extend the
same idea of an unfixed recipe furthest of the three drinks in this group,
including versions built on mulled cider or on apple juice with no alcohol
in it at all <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
