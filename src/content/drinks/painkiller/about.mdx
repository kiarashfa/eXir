The drink is placed at the Soggy Dollar Bar, a beach bar on White Bay,
Jost Van Dyke, in the British Virgin Islands, reachable only by swimming in
from anchored boats <Cite ref="gotrum"/>. Its invention is credited to
Daphne Henderson, the bar's owner through the 1970s, though at least one
account from a former staff member disputes this and credits George Myrick,
who ran the neighbouring Sandcastle bar before Henderson <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
The original was built on Cruzan rum rather than the Pusser's now associated
with the name <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Charles Tobias, founder of Pusser's Rum, tried for roughly two years to get
Henderson's recipe from her directly and was refused each time
<Cite ref="gotrum"/>. He worked out his own version by taste rather than by
being given the formula, and it was that reconstruction — not Henderson's
original — that Pusser's registered as a US trademark on both the name and
the recipe in 1989 <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

That trademark had a real consequence decades later: when a New York bar
named itself Painkiller in 2011 and served the drink with rums other than
Pusser's, the company issued a cease-and-desist, and the dispute was settled
out of court only once the bar agreed to rebrand as PKNY <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
Bartenders in several cities responded by boycotting Pusser's Rum over the
dispute <Cite ref="wiki"/>. The ratio at the centre of both the original and
the trademarked version — pineapple juice at four parts to one part each of
cream of coconut and orange juice, over rum and crushed ice with grated
nutmeg — is the one detail every account agrees on <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
