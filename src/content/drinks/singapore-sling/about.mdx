The drink is credited to Ngiam Tong Boon, a Chinese-born bartender working the
Long Bar of Raffles Hotel in Singapore sometime between 1899 and 1915
<Cite ref="difford"/>. The hotel's own account narrows this to 1915
specifically and states that Ngiam died later the same year <Cite ref="raffles"/>,
which leaves the drink's credited inventor no time to have refined it himself.

No recipe in Ngiam's own hand survives. The earliest documented text is a note
scrawled by a visitor to the bar in 1936 <Cite ref="wiki"/>, and Difford's Guide
records that no authentic version from before it survived the Japanese
occupation of Singapore in 1942 <Cite ref="difford"/>. What the hotel now serves
as the original recipe was reconstructed well after the drink's own invention,
and critics have pointed at the timing: the version in current use dates to a
1970s relaunch of the Long Bar, an era when pink, fruit-forward tiki-style
drinks were in fashion, and the recipe that resulted is heavy with pineapple
juice and grenadine in exactly that register <Cite ref="difford"/>. Whether
those two ingredients were part of Ngiam's own drink or added to suit the
fashion of the relaunch is disputed rather than settled.

A narrower dispute concerns the cherry component alone: published accounts
differ on whether the original called for a sweetened cherry liqueur or a
clear, dry cherry eau-de-vie, kirschwasser <Cite ref="difford"/>. The name took
almost as long to settle as the recipe. Early references describe the drink as
a "Straits Sling" or plainly a "Gin Sling," with "Singapore Sling" becoming the
fixed name only across the 1920s and into the 1930s <Cite ref="difford"/>.

The scale of the disagreement is old news within cocktail writing rather than a
modern discovery: no two of the recipes published for this drink have matched,
an observation put in print by D. A. Embury in *The Fine Art of Mixing Drinks*
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. The version served today, at Raffles and everywhere else, is
a later reconstruction of a drink whose credited inventor left nothing written
down.
