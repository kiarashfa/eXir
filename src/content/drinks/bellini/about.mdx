The drink is credited to Giuseppe Cipriani, founder of Harry's Bar on the edge
of Venice's Grand Canal <Cite ref="difford"/>. He combined fresh-pressed white
peach with Prosecco, drawing on an older Italian habit of marinating peaches
in wine, and named the result after Giovanni Bellini, the 15th-century
Venetian painter whose canvases favoured the same pink the drink turned out
<Cite ref="difford"/>.

The date of the first pour is not settled. Difford's Guide places it in 1945,
fourteen years after Cipriani opened the bar <Cite ref="difford"/>, while other
accounts put the invention anywhere from the mid-1930s to 1948, with no
source giving a more exact date <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

The drink began as a summer-only specialty, limited by the short season for
fresh white peaches; it became available year-round only once a supplier
began shipping peach puree to Harry's Bar and its later New York branch
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. Its short list of ingredients has since been carried into
a family of related drinks at the same bar and elsewhere, substituting other
purees for the peach — strawberry for a Rossini, raspberry for a Tintoretto
— none of which are the Bellini itself.
