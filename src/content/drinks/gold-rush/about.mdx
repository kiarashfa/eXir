The drink was created around 2000 at Milk & Honey, Sasha Petraske's
Lower East Side speakeasy, and is credited to T.J. Siegal, a regular at the
bar rather than one of its bartenders <Cite ref="punch"/>. Siegal asked for
his usual bourbon sour made instead with a honey syrup that bar had recently
started keeping on hand <Cite ref="punch"/>. That syrup, mixed roughly three
parts honey to one part water by Milk & Honey bartender Toby Maloney, had
been developed for a different drink, the Honeysuckle, a honey-based riff on
the daiquiri, and found its lasting use in Siegal's sour instead
<Cite ref="punch"/>.

The result is described in both accounts as a direct variation on the
whiskey sour, with honey syrup standing in for the plain sugar syrup and
nothing else in the formula changed <Cite ref="tuxedo"/>. It was not the
bar's most requested drink in its early years — the Mojito, the Queen's Park
Swizzle and the Bee's Knees were all ordered more often at the time — but it
outlasted them in wider circulation <Cite ref="punch"/>. Its spread beyond
Milk & Honey is credited in large part to Jim Meehan, who put the recipe in
front of a wider audience through his own bar and his cocktail writing
<Cite ref="punch"/>, after which it travelled through the bars Petraske
himself went on to open. It is now poured widely enough to sit on menus
alongside the Old Fashioned and the Manhattan, drinks many decades older than
it <Cite ref="punch"/>.
