The bartender Katie Stipe created the Siesta around 2006 at Flatiron Lounge
in Manhattan <Cite ref="diffords"/>. It is built on the same double-citrus
shake as the Hemingway Daiquiri — lime paired against a second, softer
citrus, sweetened and shaken — but swaps that drink's white rum for blanco
tequila and its maraschino liqueur for a smaller measure of Campari
<Cite ref="punch"/>. The result is usually described as sitting between
three earlier drinks at once, carrying something of the Hemingway
Daiquiri's citrus shake, the Paloma's grapefruit and tequila, and the
Margarita's base spirit, rather than descending cleanly from any single one
of them <Cite ref="diffords"/>.

Campari's role in the recipe is deliberately restrained: it is present in a
small enough measure to read as bitterness on the finish rather than to
dominate the colour or the aroma, which keeps the drink closer to a tequila
sour with an edge than to an Americano-style bitter cocktail
<Cite ref="punch"/>. That restraint is part of what distinguishes it from
other tequila-and-Campari drinks that lean further toward the bitter side of
the pairing.
