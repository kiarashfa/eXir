Documentary evidence places the name in Sydney by the early 1980s: a May 1983
newspaper review of the café Miller's Treat refers to a "flat white coffee" on
its menu <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. Alan Preston put the name on the menu at his
own Moors Espresso Bar in Sydney in 1985 and later said he had brought it south
from cafés in far north Queensland <Cite ref="district"/>.

New Zealand's claim is later by the calendar but no less confidently made.
Auckland's Cafe DKD, run by Derek Townsend and Darrell Ahlers, is said to have
served a flat white as an alternative to the Italian-style lattes then
fashionable, having learned the name from someone who had worked in Sydney
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. A separate and more often repeated account places the
drink in Wellington in 1989, where barista Fraser McInnes is said to have
served a customer a cappuccino whose low-fat milk would not foam, apologised
for it, and called the result a flat white <Cite ref="district"/>.

Neither story is documented well enough to settle the question, and the
dispute itself is now part of the drink's public identity: it resurfaced in
2015 when a large chain's press materials credited Australia by name, which
New Zealand's press disputed in turn <Cite ref="district"/>. District
Magazine's own assessment is that both accounts rest on personal recollection
rather than contemporary record, and that the debate is "too obscured by
history for anyone to win" <Cite ref="district"/>. This page does not choose
between them.

What is agreed is the drink itself. It is built from a double shot and a
shorter pour of steamed milk than a latte takes, finished as a single glossy
layer of microfoam rather than the cappuccino's thick, spoonable foam
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/> — a smaller, stronger cup than either of its more
famous milk-forward relatives, and one built around the espresso instead of
around the milk.
