The recipe served today is credited to Robert "Rosebud" Butt, who says he
made it in 1972 at the OBI East in Hampton Bays, New York, as his entry in a
bartending contest that challenged roughly twenty competitors to build a
drink around triple sec <Cite ref="dans"/>. Butt won, and the version he
entered — vodka, gin, white rum, tequila and triple sec, lengthened with
lemon juice and cola — is the one that spread from Long Island outward
<Cite ref="dans"/>.

The name is contested by an unrelated, older drink from a different Long
Island. Southern lore credits a Prohibition-era bootlegger, Charles "Old
Man" Bishop of Long Island, Tennessee, with a 1920s drink of the same name,
built from whiskey, rum, vodka, gin and tequila sweetened with maple syrup
rather than citrus and cola <Cite ref="dans"/>. Tourism boards representing
the two towns later staged a publicity contest to settle which drink had the
better claim to the name; Long Island, New York's side lost and called the
result rigged <Cite ref="dans"/>. The two recipes remain genuinely
different drinks that happen to share a name, not variations of one
another.

The name itself describes the drink's colour rather than its contents: built
correctly, the five spirits and cola turn the same amber as brewed iced tea,
and no tea is anywhere in the glass <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
