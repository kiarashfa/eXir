The drink is associated above all with Punjab, in northern India, where
yoghurt fermentation techniques are traced back to the Indus Valley
Civilisation and lassi itself is described by some historians as dating to
around 1000 BCE, early enough that it is sometimes called the original
smoothie <Cite ref="tastingtable"/>. Its documented history in English is far
more recent and much better established: the word "lassi" is first recorded
in English in 1882, borrowed from Hindi, though the word's own deeper
etymology remains disputed between two proposed Sanskrit roots
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

From a single base of yoghurt loosened with water, the drink splits into two
genuinely different directions rather than one recipe with an optional
sweetener. A sweet version is built with sugar and aromatics such as
cardamom, rosewater and saffron; a salted version replaces the sugar with
salt and savoury spices, most often black pepper and toasted cumin
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. Fruit versions, mango above all, are a further
branch rather than a third base — mango is native to the Indian
subcontinent and was very likely among the earliest fruits added, which is
part of why it remains the most widely recognised version outside India
<Cite ref="tastingtable"/>.

The two versions on this page represent that same split rather than one
drink and a variation on it: sugar and cardamom on one side, salt and cumin
on the other, both blended from the same base to the same froth. Neither is
a simplified or reduced form of the other, and a reader used to only the
sweet version is often surprised that the salted one is not a savoury
novelty but the older, plainer register of the same drink.
