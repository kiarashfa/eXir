The shaken Margarita is older and its own origin is disputed elsewhere; the
frozen version has a single, well-documented inventor and an exact date. On
11 May 1971, Mariano Martinez served the first batch from a machine at his
restaurant, Mariano's Mexican Cuisine, near Southern Methodist University in
Dallas <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>. His father's own Margarita recipe, carried
from a San Antonio speakeasy where he had worked in 1938, was popular enough
that a single bartender blending drinks to order could not keep pace with
demand <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>.

Martinez's fix was mechanical rather than culinary: a secondhand soft-serve
ice cream machine, bought and modified over several months to hold a
margarita mix at a slush consistency instead of freezing it solid
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. Getting the texture right took adjusting the recipe itself,
including added sugar, so that the mixture would set into a uniform slush in
the machine rather than separating into ice and liquid <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>.
The timing mattered as much as the machine: Texas had only legalized selling
liquor by the drink in 1970, opening the market a frozen, consistent cocktail
could be sold into at volume <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

The machine spread fast through Dallas and then nationally, carrying the
frozen Margarita out of Tex-Mex restaurants and into the wider cocktail
repertoire. In 2005, the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History
acquired Martinez's original machine, judging it significant enough to the
country's food and drink history to preserve <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>
<Cite ref="wiki"/>.
