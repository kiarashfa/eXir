Australian bartender Sam Ross created the drink in 2005 while working at
Milk & Honey in New York <Cite ref="wiki"/>. It began as a variation on the
Gold Rush, an earlier Milk & Honey drink built from bourbon, honey syrup and
lemon juice <Cite ref="coeno"/>. Ross replaced the bourbon with a mellow
blended Scotch, cut the honey syrup with fresh ginger juice, and finished the
drink with a float of Islay single malt poured over the top, adding a
different bottle at a different point in the same build rather than changing
the base spirit outright <Cite ref="coeno"/>.

The name refers to the antibiotic, discovered by the Scottish scientist
Alexander Fleming, and trades on the drink's honey, ginger and Scotch reading
like a cold remedy rather than on any actual medicinal claim
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. The float is the detail most often singled out: tasted
before the rest of the drink has had a chance to mix in, it delivers a
concentrated hit of peat smoke that softens sip by sip as the layers merge
<Cite ref="coeno"/>.

The drink spread from New York to London and from there to bars worldwide,
and the writer Robert Simonson has called it the most widely travelled new
cocktail of the twenty-first century <Cite ref="coeno"/>. By 2024 it ranked
among the most commonly ordered cocktails globally, nineteen years after it
was first poured <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
