The bartender J. "Popo" Galsini built the drink at the Kona Kai resort in
Huntington Beach, California, where Douglas Aircraft engineers working on
the experimental X-15 rocket plane drank, and named it the X-15 in their
honour <Cite ref="coldglass"/>. After the X-15 test pilot Michael Adams was
killed in a crash in October 1967, Galsini renamed the drink Saturn, for the
rocket that would carry the Apollo programme to the moon three weeks later
<Cite ref="coldglass"/>.

Under its new name, the drink went on to win the International Bartenders
Association's World Cocktail Championship in 1967 <Cite ref="almanac"/>. It
is gin-based, an unusual choice for a tiki drink of the period, when rum was
the default, and it carries no rum in it at all <Cite ref="almanac"/>. Its
garnish is part of the design: a strip of lemon peel wound around a cherry to
suggest a ringed planet floating on the surface, in keeping with the drink's
name.

The recipe fell out of circulation after Galsini's competition win and was
not generally known until the tiki historian Jeff Berry found it recorded on
a commemorative bartenders' guild glass and published it in *Beachbum Berry
Remixed* <Cite ref="coldglass"/>. Berry's published version is the one
generally followed today, and it is the one this page follows.
