No single origin is settled. Several bartenders working independently through
the 1980s converged on close variants of the same pink, citrus-vodka sour, and
each has a documented claim.

Neal Murray dates his version to autumn 1975, mixed at a Minneapolis
steakhouse from a Cape Cod and a Kamikaze; a regular's remark, "how
cosmopolitan," gave it a name he later carried to San Francisco
<Cite ref="diffords"/>. Cheryl Cook made a citron-vodka, triple sec and
cranberry version at The Strand in Miami's South Beach around 1988, and the
name took hold locally after her bar appeared on a 1989 cover of *Cosmopolitan*
magazine <Cite ref="diffords"/>. In New York, bartender Melissa Huffsmith
brought a version of the San Francisco drink to The Odeon in Tribeca, where
Toby Cecchini reworked it in 1988 into close to the recipe served today —
Absolut Citron, Cointreau, fresh lime juice and Ocean Spray cranberry juice
<Cite ref="punch"/>.

Dale DeGroff put his own recipe on the menu at the Rainbow Room in 1996 and
was, for a time, credited by *New York* magazine as the drink's inventor, a
claim he has explicitly declined <Cite ref="punch"/>. His version thinned the
Cointreau and lime and added more cranberry, closer to the pale pink most
readers now expect, and introduced the flamed orange peel some bars still use
as a garnish <Cite ref="punch"/>.

The vodka used throughout this early history was citron (lemon-flavoured)
rather than plain, a distinction the drink's later popularity mostly erased as
it spread onto menus built around a single house vodka.
