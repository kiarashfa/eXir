The drink dates to the mid-1930s, when child actress Shirley Temple was at
the height of her fame, but its exact origin is contested among at least
three places: Chasen's and the Brown Derby in Los Angeles, and the Royal
Hawaiian Hotel in Honolulu, all have a claim to having invented it <Cite ref="wiki"/>
<Cite ref="foxnews"/>. The most commonly repeated version of the story holds
that a bartender mixed ginger ale, grenadine and a cherry for the young star
so that she would have something to drink that looked like the cocktails
the adults at her table were having <Cite ref="foxnews"/>.

Shirley Temple herself, by then a former actress serving as a US
ambassador and using her married name, Shirley Temple Black, weighed in
directly in a 1986 radio interview. Asked about the drink, she credited the
Brown Derby specifically, said plainly that she "had nothing to do with it,"
and added that she disliked it, calling it "saccharine sweet" and "icky"
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. She noted with some resignation that she was served the
drink everywhere she went regardless <Cite ref="wiki"/>.

The classic build — ginger ale, grenadine and a maraschino cherry — has
picked up regional variation over time; some versions substitute a lemon-
lime soda for the ginger ale <Cite ref="foxnews"/>.
