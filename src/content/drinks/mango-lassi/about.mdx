Lassi is a yoghurt drink from Punjab, traditionally made by thinning yoghurt
with water rather than milk <Cite ref="wiki"/>. Food writing routinely dates it
to around 1000 BCE <Cite ref="tasting"/>, but the word itself is documented far
more recently: the earliest attestation on record is from 1882, and its own
derivation is disputed between two Sanskrit roots, one meaning stickiness and
the other juice <Cite ref="wiki"/>. The gap between the popular date and the
linguistic one is typical of drinks assumed to be as old as the food culture
around them, without a text or a name to fix the assumption to.

The mango version is a later addition to an older base rather than a separate
tradition. Mango is native across South and Southeast Asia, which is usually
given as the reason it became an early and lasting flavour once fruit started
being blended into lassi at all <Cite ref="tasting"/>, though no source dates
that step specifically. Plain and salted lassi remain the everyday forms
across the region; the sweetened mango version is the one that travelled
furthest outside it and is now the most widely recognised abroad
<Cite ref="tasting"/>.

Both forms share the same practical origin as a way of using yoghurt before
refrigeration, and the same traditional role: a cooling drink in a hot
climate, taken as a snack or after a spiced meal rather than with one
<Cite ref="tasting"/>.
