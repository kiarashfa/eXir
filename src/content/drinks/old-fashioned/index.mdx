<Step id="chill">
  Chill a rocks glass and set a single large cube in it. One large piece
  melts more slowly than the same mass broken into small ones, which matters
  more here than in any stirred-and-strained relative, because this cube
  keeps working for as long as the drink is on the table.
</Step>

<Step id="combine">
  Pour the <Qty ref="syrup"/> and the <Qty ref="bitters"/> over the cube,
  then add the <Qty ref="bourbon"/>.
</Step>

<Step id="stir">
  Stir for <Dur step="stir"/>, just enough to marry the syrup and bitters
  into the spirit and start the cube working. It is a shorter stir than a
  strained drink gets, because there is no second glass to bring it to
  equilibrium in — the rest happens in the glass the drinker is holding.
</Step>

<Step id="garnish">
  Cut a <Len cm={4}/> swathe of orange peel, hold it skin-outwards over the
  surface and squeeze so the oil lands on the drink. Rub it around the rim
  and drop it in.
</Step>
