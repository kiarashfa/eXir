The earliest printed recipe does not introduce the drink on its own terms.
O. H. Byron's 1884 guide defines it in five words, as the same as the
Manhattan with gin substituted for whisky <Cite ref="beeretseq"/>. That
the Manhattan needed no further explanation by then suggests it was already the
better known of the two, though the record does not settle which came first.

Two rival invention stories exist, and neither survives close reading. One
credits Jerry Thomas, working at San Francisco's Occidental Hotel; the other
credits an unnamed bartender in the town of Martinez, California, mixing a
drink for a traveller headed there. Thomas's own guide is the problem for the
first story: the recipe appears only in the 1887 edition, printed two years
after his death, so it cannot be evidence of what he served in his lifetime
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

That 1887 text calls for a pony of Old Tom gin, a full glass of vermouth, two
dashes of maraschino and a dash of Boker's bitters <Cite ref="wikipedia"/> — a
sweeter, more heavily bittered drink than most modern pours. The base spirit
is less settled than the name suggests, too: through the middle of the
nineteenth century the port of New York was clearing hundreds of times more
Dutch genever than English gin, so an early bartender's "gin" more plausibly
meant genever than the London dry style the name calls up now
<Cite ref="diffords"/>.

Whether the Martini is a simplified descendant of this drink is itself
contested rather than settled. Genealogies drawn from the period's guides
describe a family of near-identical drinks — Martine, Martineau, Martigny
among them — circulating under slightly different names in different cities,
which reads less like one drink evolving into another and more like the same
sweet-gin-and-sweet-vermouth idea passed from bar to bar and relabelled each
time it changed hands <Cite ref="herzog"/>. The Martini most likely shares a
grandparent with this drink rather than descending from it directly, which is
closer to the truth than either one's usual telling admits.
