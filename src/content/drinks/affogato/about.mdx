The name is a past participle: *affogato* is Italian for "drowned", describing
what the hot espresso does to the gelato underneath it rather than naming an
inventor or a place. No specific origin has been established, and the two
stories usually offered do not agree with each other <Cite ref="tastingtable"/>.
One credits a medieval friar named Angelico, in Piedmont, with spontaneously
pouring coffee over ice cream; the other places its emergence in Italian café
culture generally, sometime in the middle of the twentieth century, once
espresso machines and modern gelato-making had both become common fixtures of
an Italian bar <Cite ref="tastingtable"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. The friar
story has the shape of the kind of origin legend that attaches itself after
the fact to a dish too simple to have needed inventing, and neither source
treats it as established.

What can be dated with more confidence is the word's entry into English
reference works, and even that is inconsistent between accounts: one source
gives 1988, another 1992 <Cite ref="tastingtable"/> <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.
Either way, the gap between a mid-century Italian café habit and an English
dictionary entry decades later tracks the spread of espresso-bar culture
outside Italy more than it tracks the dessert's actual age.

The construction itself needs nothing invented: espresso bars kept both
gelato and coffee on hand from early in the twentieth century, and pouring one
over the other is not a technique so much as an observation. It is usually
served in a tall, narrow-based glass so the gelato pools and melts at the
bottom rather than spreading thin, and it is eaten immediately, before the
espresso's heat and the gelato's cold have fully traded places
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.
