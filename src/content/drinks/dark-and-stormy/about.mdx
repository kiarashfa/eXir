Gosling Brothers has distributed rum in Bermuda since the mid-19th century,
and its own account places the drink's creation on the island shortly after
the end of the First World War, combining its Black Seal rum with a locally
made ginger beer <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

What is not in question is the trademark. Gosling Brothers holds a United
States registration on the name, dating to 1991, that specifies the drink as
one and a half ounces of Gosling's Black Seal rum topped with ginger beer in
a highball glass <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>. The company has used that
registration to challenge other producers marketing a rum as suited to a
"Dark 'n' Stormy," including litigation against a rival spirits company and
a cease-and-desist letter sent to a cocktail blog over a suggested
substitution <Cite ref="smithsonian"/>. Some bars and writers now describe
the same ginger-beer-and-dark-rum highball made with any other rum under a
different name, such as "safe harbor," specifically to stay clear of the
mark <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

The result is a drink whose name is legally, rather than just conventionally,
tied to one producer's rum — an unusual position for a cocktail name to be
in, and the reason a bar list will sometimes carry both this name and a
near-identical drink under another one.
