The drink has an unusually well-documented origin for its family: it was
created by bartender Phil Ward for the opening menu of Death & Co in
Manhattan's East Village in 2007 <Cite ref="deathco"/><Cite ref="diffords"/>.
Both the bar's own account and an independent guide agree on the bartender,
the bar and the year, which is a rarer alignment than it sounds for a cocktail
this recent.

The name states the reasoning rather than hiding it. Mezcal is produced
almost entirely around the city of Oaxaca, and the drink was built to put a
then-unfamiliar spirit in front of American drinkers without asking them to
give up tequila altogether — reposado tequila remains the larger of the two
measures, with mezcal added as a smaller, smoke-carrying second spirit rather
than a replacement for it <Cite ref="deathco"/>. That structure follows the
Old Fashioned's own template of a dominant spirit, a sweetener and a bitter
element, with the mezcal doing double duty as the drink's one true departure
from the shape.

The recipe has not stayed fixed since 2007. Aromatic bitters were the
original bittering agent, but cocoa or chocolate bitters have since become a
common substitution in bars serving the drink, a change credited to how well
that flavour sits alongside a smoky spirit rather than to any correction of
the original <Cite ref="diffords"/>. The version served here keeps the
original aromatic bitters rather than the later substitution, and finishes on
a flamed orange twist, expressed and dropped in as both the bar's own
published method and the independent recipe describe <Cite ref="deathco"/>.
