The Fog Cutter is most closely associated with Victor Bergeron, the founder
of the Trader Vic's chain, whose recipe for it appears in his 1947
*Bartender's Guide* and again, unchanged, in his 1972 revision
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. Bergeron's own note on the drink, printed alongside the
recipe, called it "delicious but a triple threat," warning that a
fruit-forward glass this size hides three separate spirits underneath it
<Cite ref="wiki"/>.

Attribution is not settled beyond that. Other tiki-era accounts credit a
version of the same drink to Don the Beachcomber's, built to the same
proportions but naming a specific gold rum, pisco brandy and Plymouth gin
rather than the generic spirits Bergeron's recipe calls for
<Cite ref="forgotten"/>. Both bars operated in the same competitive
mid-century Californian tiki scene that produced the Mai Tai dispute, where
more than one bar has a documented claim on a shared drink and no
contemporary record settles which came first.

The float of sherry on top is the drink's most distinctive feature against
other rum-forward tiki cocktails of the period, most of which build their
final layer from a stronger overproof rum rather than a fortified wine
<Cite ref="forgotten"/>. Served over ice in a tall glass with citrus, brandy,
gin and rum underneath it, the drink is built to be tasted through the
sherry first.
