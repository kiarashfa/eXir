Mixing red wine with cola predates the name: the combination was already
known in the Basque Country from the 1920s, and spread further after
Coca-Cola began bottling in Spain in 1953 <Cite ref="wikipedia"/>. The name
itself dates to a specific event, a 1972 festival in Algorta, in the
Basque town of Getxo, where organisers had laid in a large stock of red
wine that turned out to taste poor, oxidised or otherwise off
<Cite ref="vinepair"/>. Rather than waste it, stallholders cut it with cola
to mask the flaw, and the mixture caught on with the crowd
<Cite ref="wikipedia"/>.

The name is a portmanteau of two people's nicknames: Kalimero, after one of
the festival's organisers, and Motxo, the Basque word for ugly, applied to
him or to a friend depending on which telling is followed
<Cite ref="vinepair"/>. Both sources agree on the shape of the story even
where they differ on which name attached to which person, which is more
corroboration than most cocktail-naming stories get.

The drink is served in equal parts wine and cola over ice, a ratio unusual
enough among mixed drinks that it is part of the definition rather than an
option, and it remains closely associated with Basque festival culture
rather than with bar service generally.
