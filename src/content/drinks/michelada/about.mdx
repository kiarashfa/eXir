The Michelada grew out of an older and simpler drink, the chelada: beer
served with lime and salt, a habit said to have started with drinkers
wiping the rust from bottle caps with a lime before squeezing the juice
straight into the beer <Cite ref="beer52"/>. "Chela" is Mexican slang for a
cold beer, and "chelada" itself is read as a contraction of *cerveza
helada*, iced beer <Cite ref="beer52"/>.

The more elaborate Michelada is usually dated to the 1970s, in a story
centred on Michel Espér, a member of the Club Deportivo Potosino in San
Luis Potosí, who is said to have ordered his beer with lime, salt and hot
sauce after a tennis match; other club members began calling the order
"Michel's iced one," which shortened over time to Michelada <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
This is described as the most widely repeated version of the story rather
than a confirmed one <Cite ref="beer52"/>. A rival, purely linguistic
account treats the name as a portmanteau of *mi chela helada*, "my ice-cold
beer," with no named inventor at all <Cite ref="wiki"/>; the two
explanations are not reconciled by either source.

Clamato-based variants developed alongside the plainer lime-and-salt style
and are closer in shape to a Bloody Mary: a version sometimes called Leon
Rojo combines lager with Clamato, Worcestershire sauce and other savoury
seasoning, served in a chilli-salted glass <Cite ref="beer52"/>. The Cubana
style served on this page follows that same tomato-and-Worcestershire
direction rather than the plain lime-and-hot-sauce original.
