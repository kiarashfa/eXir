The name predates the drink served under it today. An earlier Tequila
Sunrise, closer to a tequila daisy of tequila, creme de cassis and lime, is
placed by spirits historian Dave Wondrich at Agua Caliente, a Prohibition-era
resort in Tijuana that drew Hollywood visitors across the border to drink
legally <Cite ref="tales"/>; a separate account credits the same period's
Arizona Biltmore, naming bartender Gene Sulit and adding soda water to the
formula <Cite ref="wiki"/>. Neither claim is treated as settled over the
other.

The orange-and-grenadine drink now known by the name was made at the
Trident, a Sausalito restaurant and bar frequented by musicians, where
bartenders Bobby Lozoff and Billy Rice dropped the cassis for grenadine
<Cite ref="wiki"/>. The bar's own house style favoured improvising drinks
loosely rather than following fixed recipes <Cite ref="tales"/>. The
resulting sunrise-coloured drink reached a wide audience in 1972, when Mick
Jagger was served one at a Trident party marking the start of the Rolling
Stones' American tour and the band kept drinking them on the road, a tour
Keith Richards later nicknamed after it <Cite ref="tales"/>. Jose Cuervo
printed the recipe on its own bottle labels the following year, and the
Eagles' 1973 song of the same name fixed the drink further in the culture of
the period <Cite ref="wiki"/>.
