Everything else in this book is a set of proportions. This family is a set of
proportions and a texture, and the texture is the harder half. Two people can
follow the same frozen daiquiri recipe and produce a slush and a soup, because
the recipe does not contain the ratio that actually decides the outcome: how
much water is present, and how much of it is frozen at the moment it is poured.

The **liquid** slot is what the blade needs to move. A blender does not cut
through a solid mass; it needs a vortex, and a vortex needs enough liquid to
form. Under-liquid the jug and the blade carves a cavity, spins in air and
warms everything around it while appearing to work. This is the most common
failure in the family and it looks like a blender problem rather than a recipe
one.

The **frozen solid** is the family's defining slot and the reason it is a family
at all. Ice, frozen fruit and ice cream are doing the same structural job:
entering as a solid and leaving as suspended crystals fine enough to drink. What
distinguishes them is what else they bring. Ice brings only water, which is why
a frozen cocktail needs to be built stronger and sweeter than its shaken
counterpart to survive it. Frozen fruit brings sugar, acid, fibre and colour, so
a smoothie needs almost nothing added. Ice cream brings fat, sugar and air, and
the air is why a milkshake is thick at a much lower solid fraction than anything
else here.

The **sweetener** is larger than instinct suggests. Sweetness perception falls
sharply with temperature, and a drink served below zero reads perhaps a third
less sweet than the same liquid at room temperature. A frozen drink adjusted to
taste while it is still liquid in the jug will always be under-sweetened by the
time it is in the glass, so the correction is made in the recipe rather than at
the end.

Two things do not appear in the formula and decide the result anyway. The first
is blending time: every second the blender runs adds heat, so the technique is
short bursts at full power rather than a long gentle one, and a drink blended
twice as long is not smoother, it is warmer. The second is service. Nothing in
this book is as time-limited — a frozen drink has a matter of minutes before it
separates into slush and water, which is why the glass is chilled first and why
this family is unbatchable in the ordinary sense. What can be prepared ahead is
the mix; the freezing is the last thing that happens.

The members split along one line: whether the frozen solid was ever going to be
tasted. In the frozen cocktails it was not, and the ice is a texture with a cost
attached. In the smoothies, lassis and milkshakes it is the drink itself, and
everything else in the jug is there to let it pour.
